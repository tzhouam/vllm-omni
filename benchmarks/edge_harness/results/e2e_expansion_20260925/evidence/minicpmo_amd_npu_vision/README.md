# MiniCPM-o 4.5 vision-stage AMD NPU feasibility on HX370

**Disposition (2026-09-25): component C placement with failed held-out numerics; the PC AMD NPU MiniCPM-o cell remains NOT E2E.** The real checkpoint at revision `503e754207c94da6bb26850b4469f367c9ea3582` supplied a BF16 SigLIP vision transformer. The retained [red-square image](../../../e2e_expansion_20260923/evidence/minicpmo_wsl_image/red_square.png) was normalized to the model processor's 448-pixel, 32×32-patch bucket. The [export script](../../../../experiments/probe_minicpmo_vision_npu.py) pins the config, source and weight-shard hashes. It isolates a vision encoder block after patch/position embedding and before post-layernorm, so image preprocessing, resampling, the thinker, speech head and vocoder are outside the measured graph.

The four-layer [FP32 export](export_report.json) used a fixed `[1,1024,1152]` embedding and matched source FP32 Torch at **1.23e-6 relative L2** on ONNX Runtime CPU. Converting this BF16 checkpoint stage to FP32 already changed its four-layer output by **1.079% relative L2** against BF16 Torch. The 243,863,528-byte ONNX artifact remains outside Git at `/home/zhout/project/edge_infer/models/minicpmo_vision_prefix4_448_20260925.onnx`, SHA-256 `bd3e7802a78a930c5cd3e8e1a9888ab73e3fa865d6f831e492e169cdd24540be`. The exact generated input and reference are retained locally in `fixture.npz`, SHA-256 `9e58e815ccf46039b28d70f1b952f79ccc27b97a57ab44eaed50bd5218f6bcd2`; rerunning the export regenerates them from pinned source files.

The [native Windows quantization/probe script](../../../../experiments/probe_minicpmo_vision_npu_candidate.py) used one image activation for A16W8 per-channel MatMul/Gemm calibration. The [first four-layer candidate](full4_quantized_probe.json) placed **20 VitisAI node events per call** (400 over 20 calls) but missed the provisional 1% candidate-versus-FP32 numerical gate: **1.048%** on both ORT CPU and NPU. Its NPU output was within **0.0564%** of the same QDQ graph on CPU. That is an artifact-specific numerical failure, not a claim that the model or NPU cannot execute vision.

The [selective candidate](first3_quantized_probe.json) left the fourth layer's eight MatMul operations FP32 on CPU. It placed **15 VitisAI node events per call** (300 over 20) and passed the same fixed-input provisional FP32 gate at **0.911%** output relative L2; NPU-versus-QDQ-CPU error was **0.0541%**. Native Windows 11 build 26200, NPU driver `32.0.203.329`, ONNX Runtime `1.30.0` and VitisAI EP `1.8.63.0` are fixed in the [environment record](environment.json). Its nearest-rank 20-call p50/p95, including the first inference after session creation, was **234.95/250.78 ms** with VitisAI versus **567.05/576.19 ms** for the same QDQ graph on ORT CPU. Session creation took **14.92 s** on the NPU and is excluded from those call timings. These are isolated, same-process component calls; Omni dispatch, downstream execution, transfer, shared-RAM admission and power are excluded. The [raw first candidate trace](full4_npu_profile.json.gz), [raw selective trace](first3_npu_profile.json.gz) and [selective worker log](first3_worker.log) are retained. Each gzip file decompresses to the SHA-256 in its corresponding probe report.

An independent [blue-circle/green-triangle image](heldout_blue_circle.png) then supplied a held-out input at the same fixed shape. Its [FP32 export and BF16 reference](heldout_export_report.json) reproduced the exact four-layer ONNX graph hash without recalibration. Reusing the selective candidate gave **6.864% relative L2 versus FP32** and **6.929% versus BF16** in the [held-out NPU report](heldout_probe.json), decisively failing the 1% gate. NPU remained within **0.0492%** of the same QDQ graph on CPU and still placed 15 VitisAI nodes per call, so the evidence points to the single-image quantization rather than NPU execution. The [raw held-out placement trace](heldout_npu_profile.json.gz) and [worker log](heldout_worker.log) are retained. The calibration-image pass must not be generalized to a vision component pass.

Recalibrating that same three-layer cut on both red-square and blue-circle activations produced a new pinned A16W8 candidate (SHA-256 `5e3d0ed188db01c643628411725614fd82927bed2a76f93a370085f49c5f2624`). It passed the provisional FP32 boundary gate on both calibration inputs: [red-square NPU](twoimage_primary_probe.json) **0.911%** and [blue-circle NPU](twoimage_blue_probe.json) **0.926%** relative L2. A third, unseen [gradient-grid image](heldout_gradient_grid.png) gave a [held-out NPU result](twoimage_third_probe.json) of **1.579%** versus FP32 and **1.878%** versus BF16, failing the gate. All three evaluations placed 15 VitisAI nodes per call over 20 calls; on the third image, NPU differed only **0.0400%** from QDQ CPU. The [raw red](twoimage_primary_profile.json.gz), [blue](twoimage_blue_profile.json.gz) and [third-image](twoimage_third_profile.json.gz) traces, corresponding [third-image export](third_export_report.json), and [worker logs](twoimage_third_worker.log) are retained. This two-image calibration improves its included images but still does not generalize to an independent image.

To test the full vision transformer handoff, the [same pinned weights](layer3_red_export.json) were also exported at the earlier **three-layer** boundary (182,897,665-byte ONNX, SHA-256 `b68fe07f9ae74d2c4d3a953b43ce5b33e28f86a41f413f857d2e00edf67b9efb`). A two-image A16W8 candidate (SHA-256 `bba44f82fff3964ff8c3d2470e45f2fb529bc4e049a098a0c9bfef75c0fae110`) put 15 VitisAI nodes per call on the HX370 NPU, leaving **layers 4–27 and post-layernorm in unchanged BF16 Torch on CPU**. The [red 20-call probe](layer3_red_probe.json) measured nearest-rank p50/p95 **156.81/176.67 ms** on NPU versus **467.00/475.76 ms** for its QDQ CPU graph, after a **13.65 s** NPU session creation. The [blue](layer3_blue_probe.json) and [third-image](layer3_third_probe.json) captures are one NPU call each, not latency profiles. Their [raw red](layer3_red_profile.json.gz), [blue](layer3_blue_profile.json.gz) and [third](layer3_third_profile.json.gz) placement traces are retained.

The measured NPU tensors are retained as [red](layer3_red_output.npz), [blue](layer3_blue_output.npz) and [third](layer3_third_output.npz) boundary artifacts. Replaying each through the exact BF16 CPU suffix with the [handoff audit](../../../../experiments/audit_minicpmo_vision_handoff.py) produced [red](layer3_handoff_red.json), [blue](layer3_handoff_blue.json) and [third](layer3_handoff_third.json) post-layernorm errors of **9.276%**, **7.660%** and **9.077%** relative L2 versus the same checkpoint's all-BF16 vision path. The unquantized FP32 boundary sent through that same BF16 suffix already differed **8.576%**, **6.672%** and **7.576%**. This control shows that converting the early checkpoint layers to FP32 is a major source of the downstream drift; the NPU candidate adds further error. The prior four-layer NPU capture with BF16 layers 5–27 had [red](handoff_red.json), [blue](handoff_blue.json) and [third](handoff_third.json) post-layernorm errors of **9.183%**, **7.747%** and **9.096%**. Moving the cut back one layer did not fix quality. These are real NPU outputs and a full SigLIP transformer/post-layernorm replay, but still exclude image resampling, thinker output, complete multimodal behavior, transfer-inclusive latency and power.

The source BF16 comparison remains a quality blocker: the selective NPU candidate differs **1.425% relative L2** from the checkpoint's BF16 four-layer output on the calibration image, without a downstream task tolerance. A separate [27-layer transformer-block FP32 export](full_export_report.json) is numerically faithful to FP32 Torch at **9.24e-6 relative L2** but differs **5.311%** from BF16 Torch on that image before quantization. Its 1,646,081,657-byte ONNX artifact remains outside Git at `/home/zhout/project/edge_infer/models/minicpmo_vision_prefix27_448_20260925.onnx`, SHA-256 `534641cb3589df399b90f78c4fb3629079f824224eb0bd65801d26725cb16134`. This graph excludes post-layernorm and resampler and was **not run on NPU**.

Next, find a BF16-aligned export/runtime or a downstream image-task tolerance that accepts the mixed precision, then calibrate on representative diverse images and require an **independent** held-out gate. The two synthetic calibration images do not satisfy that requirement. Only then test complete MiniCPM-o output, transfer-inclusive latency, shared-RAM admission, cancellation and sustained power. The earlier [NPU speech-token projection](../minicpmo_amd_npu_projection/README.md) remains a separate one-activation component; its per-token split was slower than the FP32 CPU equivalent and is not enabled by default.
